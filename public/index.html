    How it works
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Maekawa
    
          
    Where creases meet, the number of mountain folds and valley folds always differs by exactly two.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Kawasaki
    
          
    Going round that point, every other angle adds up to 180°, so the paper lands back on itself.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Big-little-big
    
          
    A wedge smaller than both its neighbours must sit between one mountain and one valley.
    
        
    
      
    
      
    Each point is then tested exactly by folding its smallest wedges away one at a time (crimping). That proves every point folds flat on its own. Whether the whole sheet does is NP-hard to decide in general, so the result covers every point, not every possible collision between distant flaps.
